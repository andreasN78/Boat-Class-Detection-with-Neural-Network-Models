How to read the pronunciation

khExactly the Greek χ (χαρά, χέρι). The most common “foreign” Hebrew sound, and you already have it.
rMade in the throat, like French r. Close to Greek γ in γάλα. Never rolled like Greek ρ.
tsGreek τσ as in τσάι.
shEnglish “sh” as in shop. Round your lips more than for σ.
hA light breath, as in English hello. Israelis often drop it in fast speech.
a e i o uWritten ah, eh, ee, oh, oo. The same five vowels as Greek α, ε, ι, ο, ου. Keep them short and clean.
ayLike Greek “έι” in κέικ: AY-foh.
'No vowel between the letters, or a tiny break. In k'TSAT the k runs straight into ts.
CAPSThe stressed syllable, like the Greek τόνος. Many Hebrew words stress the last syllable.
man / womanSome words have a form for men and a form for women. Pick yours in Settings above.

Your daily routine (15 minutes)

  1. Review the words the app asks for today. Say each one out loud before you tap Show.
  2. Learn today's lesson: play every word twice and repeat it out loud.
  3. Listen & repeat the lesson hands-free, for example while walking.
  4. Say the sentence of the day three times, a bit faster each time.
  5. Press Mark day done. Its words join your reviews from tomorrow.

Daily reminders: add hebrew/hebrew-daily.ics to your phone's calendar for a 09:00 notification with each day's words.
