עברית

30 days of spoken Israeli Hebrew. Every word is written the way it sounds, with the stressed syllable highlighted, plus the meaning in Greek and English. Tap ▶ to hear it.

How to read the pronunciation

khExactly the Greek χ (χαρά, χέρι). The most common “foreign” Hebrew sound, and you already have it.
rMade in the throat, like French r. Close to Greek γ in γάλα. Never rolled like Greek ρ.
tsGreek τσ as in τσάι.
shEnglish “sh” as in shop. Round your lips more than for σ.
hA light breath, as in English hello. Israelis often drop it in fast speech.
a e i o uWritten ah, eh, ee, oh, oo. The same five vowels as Greek α, ε, ι, ο, ου. Keep them short and clean.
ayLike Greek “έι” in κέικ: AY-foh.
'A tiny break, as in English “uh-oh”. In k'TSAT the k runs straight into ts.
CAPSThe stressed syllable, like the Greek τόνος. Most Hebrew words stress the last syllable.
/Two forms: first for a man, second for a woman. Choose the one that matches you (when you talk about yourself) or the person you're talking to.

Your daily routine (15 minutes)

  1. Listen to each word twice with ▶ and repeat out loud.
  2. Say the sentence of the day three times, a bit faster each time.
  3. Test yourself: switch on Practice, look at the Greek, say the Hebrew, then tap to check.
  4. Use one word in your head during the day: say “toda” instead of “ευχαριστώ”.
  5. Press Mark day done. Every 7th day is a review of the week.

Daily reminders

Add hebrew/hebrew-daily.ics from the GitHub repo to your phone's calendar. You get a 9:00 notification each day with that day's words inside it.
